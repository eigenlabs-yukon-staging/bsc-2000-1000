# Field-cube product with two bounded searches

## Encoder and decoder

Split the message into 4 blocks with m=250 bits each.
Represent each block u in the polynomial basis of F2[X]/(P),
with bit i the coefficient of X^i. Set P=X^250 + X^59 + X^40 + X^32 + 1.
Encode u as (u,u^3) and concatenate the component codewords.
The systematic halves make this injective on 1000-bit messages
and the total number of channel uses is 2000.

The declared computation verifies X^(2^m)=X modulo P and gcd
one with X^(2^(m/p))+X for each prime divisor p of m. These prime
divisors are two and five, giving the indices 50 and 125. A root of
any irreducible factor of degree d generates a field of size 2^d.
It is fixed by the j-th Frobenius power exactly when d divides j:
Frobenius to the d-th power fixes the whole field, and no smaller
positive power does by the polynomial root bound. Thus d divides
m, and any proper divisor d would divide one of the tested m/p.
The full-degree factor exhausts P, which is therefore irreducible.

For a received component (a,b), the candidate set is all v with
wt(v xor a)<=29 or wt(v^3 xor b)<=26. Choose v
minimizing wt(v xor a)+wt(v^3 xor b), with smallest numerical
v winning a tie. Concatenate these component decisions. The
candidate set includes v=a. The program searches both parts of
the union and accounts for all valid cube roots.

## Hyperplane collision bound for each component

Let Q=2^m. For a uniform u and a fixed d!=0, the parity difference
(u+d)^3+u^3 equals d^3((u/d)^2+(u/d)+1). In K, the map
z -> z^2+z is F2-linear with kernel {0,1}, and hence has image
of size Q/2. The trace Tr(z)=sum_(i=0..m-1) z^(2^i) is F2-linear
with values in F2 since it is fixed by squaring. It is nonzero
by the polynomial root bound, and Tr(z^2+z)=0. The parity difference is
uniform on the affine hyperplane Tr(d^-3 c)=Tr(1), where
Tr(1) is m mod 2. The relation between mask weights and the
absolute ball bias below applies to either right-hand side. Its nonzero coordinate mask
has entries Tr(d^-3 X^j). The map taking a field multiplier
to that vector of trace coordinates is linear and invertible:
a nonzero multiplier whose trace coordinates were all zero
would, by invertibility of field multiplication, make Tr
zero on all K. Each multiplier d^-3 has at most three
preimages by the degree-three root bound.

Let V_l be the m-bit Hamming ball volume of radius l.
For a mask of nonzero weight w, the sum of the signs
(-1)^(mask dot e) over a centered ball of radius l is
the coefficient K_l(w) of X^l in

    (1-X)^(w-1) * (1+X)^(m-w).

Indeed the exact shell signs are generated by
(1-X)^w*(1+X)^(m-w). Summing shells through l multiplies
that polynomial by the series 1/(1-X). Translating
the ball multiplies the signed count by a common sign.
An affine hyperplane specified by that mask therefore
meets any radius-l ball in at most
(V_l+abs(K_l(w)))/2 points.

For each l, make a list of the m absolute magnitudes
abs(K_l(w)), w=1..m, with multiplicities 3*binom(m,w),
and sort by decreasing magnitude. Write H(l,c) for the
sum of the first c entries with those multiplicities.
There are at most three directions d producing any one
nonzero mask, so any at most c directions have total
imbalance at most H(l,c), even if the directions depend
on the true noise z. This is an upper envelope from all
masks and uses the actual magnitude at every mask weight.

The computation obtains all K_l(w) exactly. Put N=m-1,
g=N-2*(w-1), K_(-1)=0 and K_0=1. The coefficient
recurrence is

    (l+1)*K_(l+1) = g*K_l - (N-l+1)*K_(l-1).

For the displayed generating polynomial F, differentiating
gives (1-X^2)*F'=(g-N*X)*F. Comparing coefficients
yields this recurrence. The divisions are exact and the
computation checks them. Sorted prefix sums of the
multiplicities and of their weighted values evaluate
H(l,c) by taking full groups then a partial last group.
There are 3*(2^m-1) available multiplicities, exceeding
each capacity used below.

For total weight cap t, let E_t be the set of errors
in the union of systematic and parity supports specified
by the two radii Rs, Rp, with total weight at most t.
Let J_t=|E_t|. For a received word y let m_y count
candidates with error in E_t. Then sum_y m_y=Q*J_t.
For fixed true noise z in E_t, a competitor u+d has
first-half error weight s=wt(z_I+d), independent of
the uniform u. For s<=t the corresponding permitted
parity radius is l=t-s if s<=Rs and l=min(Rp,t-s)
otherwise. For that s there are at most n_s=binom(m,s)
nonzero directions d. The hyperplane distribution assigns
mass 2/Q per allowed parity difference. The possible
competitors with this s have expected count bounded by
(n_s*V_l+H(l,n_s))/Q. Consequently define

    J_t = sum_(s=0..t) n_s * V_l,
    A_t = sum_(s=0..t) (n_s * V_l + H(l,n_s)).

For each fixed z the expected number of distinct
competitors with error in E_t is at most A_t/Q.
Summing over z in E_t and all Q true words yields

    sum_y m_y*(m_y-1) <= J_t*A_t.

## Coverage and block success

Let F_t be the number of y with m_y>0 divided by Q.
Cauchy-Schwarz gives F_t>=J_t Q/(Q+A_t). For integer
k>=1 use (x-k)(x-k-1)>=0 at integer occupancy x>0.
Rearranging yields k(k+1)>= (2k+1)x-x^2. At x=0
the right side is zero. Summing over all received
words gives the second bound
F_t>=J_t*(2k Q-A_t)/(Q*k*(k+1)).
Take k=1+floor(A_t/Q). The experiment floors both
bounds and takes the maximum with the preceding
lower bound, producing nondecreasing D_t<=F_t,
starting at D_(-1)=0.

For a received word whose least eligible distance is w,
the joint contribution of correct decoding summed over
uniform messages is Q^-1 9^(2m-w)/10^(2m). Exactly one
message is selected, and its noise probability is the
same for any tied minimizer. Summing by winning distance
gives exact component success
sum_t (F_t-F_(t-1))9^(2m-t)/10^(2m).
The experiment takes the partial numerator with D_t
through t=90. Summation by parts shows the resulting
A=sum_(t=0..90)(D_t-D_(t-1))*9^(2m-t) is no
greater than the actual success numerator. The
coefficients on D_t are positive differences of
successive weights and one positive final weight.
Later terms in the exact success are nonnegative.

Uniform component inputs and independent channel errors
make the component success events independent. Whole
success is at least A^4/10^2000. The checker verifies
the exact bound 1-A^4/10^2000 <= 26756287227241409/50000000000000000.

## Fixed data and RAM arithmetic

All offsets are low-bit first. Let k=ceil(m/64), h=m-64(k-1),
b=ceil(m/8) and n=ceil(m/4). In the field representation
there are k words and the high word has h live bits.
Initial memory uses the specified input and output prefix.
Use one bank for the field, reused across all components.
Starting at address 48 place the k-word arrays YS, YP, KEY, SQ, CUBE, BEST,
T0, T1, T2, Y, Z and CHECK in that order. The two arrays NEXT
and LAST follow, with R+1 words each. After them come
the 16 rows of k words each of multiplication scratch
MULT. The fixed reduction tables REDUCE_1 and REDUCE_4
have lengths 2 and 16 respectively and follow MULT.
At index c, REDUCE_s holds the carryless product
of c and P_low=P+X^m. Its degree is less than 64,
including at s=4, since deg(P_low)<=59.

After those tables put b consecutive square tables, one
for each byte position, with 256 k-word entries each.
The entry d in the j-th table consists of the field
words of (d*X^(8j))^2, with byte d treated as a polynomial.
These maps are fixed by the polynomial, and the supplied
table generators determine every word. The square tables are followed by SAVED with R+1 rows
of k words. AFFINE follows and holds m direction tables,
each with ceil(m/18) slices and 262144 entries of
k words per slice. The fixed Frobenius and constant
multiplication tables described below immediately follow
the affine slices. Their final exclusive end is 3716186470,
below 2^32. The fixed data module defines the same layout. Its host-side
entry generator squares by interleaving the polynomial bits
with zeros, and reduces any high part H*X^m by XORing H times
the lower part of the modulus. Each fold decreases degree,
so the data are the same field values as ordinary polynomial
remainder. The decoder uses the counted macros below.

All fixed-range loops and literal conditions in the following
pseudocode expand at code generation time. A macro invocation
inlines its list, with no runtime call. Moves and literal
constants are free.

SHIFT_s, for literal s in {1,4}, multiplies the field value
in R0..R[k-1] by X^s. Emit the following sequence.

    c := shr(R[k-1],h-s)
    for j=k-1 down to 1:
        t := shr(R[j-1],64-s)
        R[j] := shl(R[j],s)
        R[j] := or(R[j],t)
    R0 := shl(R0,s)
    R[k-1] := and(R[k-1],2^h-1)
    adr := add(REDUCE_s,c)
    t := load(adr)
    R0 := xor(R0,t)

The descending order preserves the lower word's old bits
for each carry. The removed coefficients above m-1
are replaced by c*P_low in the low word, which performs
modular reduction. The length count is 3k+3.

SQUARE initializes k accumulators to zero. For j=0..b-1,
load KEY+floor(j/8), shift right by 8*(j mod 8), AND with
255, multiply the value by k, and add the literal base of
byte table j. This computes its dynamic entry address
in five primitives. For each word position add the literal
word index to that address, load, and XOR the value into
its accumulator. Store the accumulators to SQ at the end.
This costs b*(5+3k)+k. Field squaring is linear,
so SQ equals KEY squared. The high padding of KEY supplies
zeros if the final byte is partial.

MUL multiplies two literal field arrays into a literal destination.
The following description uses SQ and KEY as its source names
and CUBE as its destination. Substitute the desired names at
code generation. The sources may equal the destination because
no destination is written until the result is complete. First load SQ into k registers,
writing zero and SQ to rows 0 and 1 of MULT. Use SHIFT_1
three times successively to write rows 2,4,8. For each
other nonzero row d in increasing order, XOR the stored
rows for the least set bit and the remaining bits and
store the result into row d, using two literal loads,
one XOR and one store for each word. This establishes row
d as SQ times the polynomial d. Move all R registers to
zero. For j=n-1 down to 0, load KEY+floor(j/16), shift
right by 4*(j mod 16), AND with 15, multiply by k and
add the literal base of MULT to compute the row address.
Use SHIFT_4 on R, then for each word add its literal index
to the row address, load and XOR into R. This implements
Horner's rule and ignores zero padding in a partial top
nibble. Store R to CUBE at the end. The count is
3k+3*(3k+3+k)+44k+n*(5+3k+3+3k)+k.
The non-runtime table preloads and the counted MULT
scratch updates are separate.

## Search trees and forward updates

The first search uses cap Rs in the tree. In the parity
search the same tree uses cap Rp. In the program listing
below substitute the chosen literal cap for R. The stacks
have space for Rs+1 entries and Rp<=Rs.

For each component, fixed code generation computes its
two input field starts. Extract every k-word representation
directly from the specified received words. For any
contiguous field piece of width w<=64 beginning at
offset s within word j, load word j and shift right
by s if nonzero. If s+w>64 load word j+1, shift it
left by 64-s and OR it into the current result. AND
with 2^w-1 when w<64. This is at most six primitives
and extracts precisely the desired low w bits with
zero padding. For each systematic piece use two literal
stores to copy it into YS and KEY. For each parity
piece store it into YP. This uses at most 15k
primitives. Move depth to zero and BestD to 2m+1,
and store zero at NEXT[0], costing one additional
primitive.

At initialization before the first VISIT emit SQUARE
and MUL once, writing the cube of the received systematic
field to CUBE. During traversal maintain KEY and its cube
using the edge operations below. The VISIT action zeroes
Dist and Smaller with free moves. For the first search its two
candidate arrays are KEY
and CUBE. In the parity search substitute the stated candidate
arrays for these two names in the score and update sequences.
At each literal word index j, emit

    a := load(KEY+j)
    b := load(YS+j)
    t := xor(a,b)
    t := popcount(t)
    Dist := add(Dist,t)
    a2 := load(CUBE+j)
    b2 := load(YP+j)
    t := xor(a2,b2)
    t := popcount(t)
    Dist := add(Dist,t)
    b := load(BEST+j)
    lt := ltu(a,b)
    same := eq(a,b)
    Smaller := select(same,Smaller,lt)

The most significant different word determines Smaller
because the sequence goes from low to high. Compute
ltu(Dist,BestD) and eq(Dist,BestD), AND the equality
with Smaller, OR the result with the strict-improvement
flag, and select BestD according to this choice.
At each word position load KEY and BEST, select KEY
if the choice is true or BEST otherwise, and store
it at BEST. These are 18k+5 scoring primitives.
The initial BestD lets the first candidate win,
and the update selects exactly the required minimum
with numerical ties.

Each of the two searches uses its own emitted copy of
this runtime control, reached initially at its VISIT. All following
branches fall through if their condition is false.

    VISIT:
        emit the visit action for this search
    EXTEND:
        flag := eq(depth,R)
        branch if flag to BACK
        adr := add(NEXT,depth)
        i := load(adr)
        flag := eq(i,m)
        branch if flag to BACK
        j := add(i,1)
        store(adr,j)
        depth := add(depth,1)
        adr := add(NEXT,depth)
        store(adr,j)
        adr := add(LAST,depth)
        store(adr,i)
        SAVE_AND_UPDATE(i) [only in the first search]
        FLIP(i)
        jump to VISIT
    BACK:
        flag := eq(depth,0)
        branch if flag to DONE
        adr := add(LAST,depth)
        i := load(adr)
        RESTORE() [only in the first search]
        depth := sub(depth,1)
        FLIP(i)
        jump to EXTEND

FLIP is inlined at both of its locations in each tree.

    word := shr(i,6)
    off := and(i,63)
    mask := shl(1,off)
    adr := add(KEY,word)
    t := load(adr)
    t := xor(t,mask)
    store(adr,t)

On descent before FLIP, SAVE_AND_UPDATE saves the k CUBE
words in SAVED+k*depth. Emit mul and add for the row base,
and one add, load and store per word, costing 2+3k. Let e=X^i
and b be the current KEY. The cube change is

    (b+e)^3+b^3 = e*b^2 + e^2*b + e^3.

The linear column at bit j is e*X^(2*j)+e^2*X^j.
The fixed table generator enumerates them with terms
advanced by X^2 and X. Add zero columns until the width
is a multiple of 18. The entry affine_entry(i,l,d)
XORs the 18 columns selected by d in slice l, plus
e^3 if and only if l=0. Store each entry as k field words.
Its word h is at AFFINE+i*AFFINE_STRIDE+l*262144*k+d*k+h,
with AFFINE_STRIDE=ceil(m/18)*262144*k.

For the update, form the direction base with mul and add
and initialize k accumulators by loading CUBE. For each
literal slice l, load its low word of KEY, shift right
by its within-word offset, and AND with 262143. If it
crosses, load the next word, shift left and OR before
the mask. The crossing test at code generation is
(18*l mod 64)+18>64. The last slice fits in the allocated
high word and receives its unused zeros from the padded
KEY. Next multiply the key by k and add the literal
slice offset and direction base. For each word emit add,
load and XOR into its accumulator. After all slices
store the k accumulators. Thus a descent update costs

    U = 2+2k+ceil(m/18)*(6+3k)+3*crossings.

The resulting CUBE corresponds to the child reached by
the subsequent FLIP. On ascent RESTORE loads the saved
CUBE using mul and add for the row base and add, load
and store per word, at a cost of 2+3k. The following
depth decrement and FLIP restore the parent. Thus CUBE
is correct at every first-search VISIT.

Each tree visits the root and every increasing subset of
coordinate flips of cardinality at most its own cap exactly once. The parent advances its NEXT before the child
begins at the next greater coordinate. The child
records its entering flip in LAST and undoes it on
return. Thus depth is always the current cardinality,
and all flips stay in 0..m-1. The emitted control
checks and transitions account for these operations
on every path.

## Parity search and cube roots

The DONE label of the first tree points to initialization for
the parity search. Copy the k words of YP into KEY, costing
2k primitives, move zero into depth, and store zero into
NEXT[0]. Keep the incumbent from the first search. Fall through
to the second tree, capped at Rp. Each visit has a parity
candidate x in KEY, and the emitted root circuit below
computes a proposed cube root in Z. The second tree skips both
forward affine operations SAVE_AND_UPDATE and RESTORE.
Its DONE label points to the final packing code. All these
label targets use the existing tree branches.

Define Frob_j(x)=x^(2^j), a binary linear map. MAP with literal
tag j applies Frob_j. A separate tag omega, used in the
even-degree case, multiplies by the fixed field element omega.
The tables with these literal tags have the exact order MAPS
in construction.py and are assigned consecutive banks after
AFFINE. For each tag, its rows are the k field words of the
map on d*X^(16*l), for each slice l=0..ceil(m/16)-1 and each
d=0..65535, in that order. The last slice obtains its unused
bits from zero high padding in the source words. For each
literal slice, load its source word, right shift by 16 times
the position in that word, AND with 65535, multiply by k,
and add the literal slice base. Then for each destination
word add the literal field word index, load, and XOR into
an accumulator. After all slices store all k field words.
The cost is Mmap=ceil(m/16)*(5+3k)+k. This computes precisely
the desired map by linearity. COPY of a field has k literal
loads and k literal stores. MUL is the previously specified
array-parameter multiplication.

For an integer base beta and j>=1 define
E_j(beta)=1+beta+...+beta^(j-1). Given Z=Y^E_j(beta) with
beta=2^s, a doubling uses MAP(s*j,Z,T0) and MUL(Z,T0,Z).
It sends j to 2j by multiplying the original geometric
power by its shifted copy. To append one, emit MAP(s*j,Y,T0)
then MUL(Z,T0,Z) at the doubled j, and increment the compile
time index j. Both updates give the stated geometric series.
The binary digits of the target, scanned after the leading 1
from high to low, determine the exact MAP and MUL macro list,
with no runtime branch or exponent work.

Here m is even, q=2^m, and d=(q-1)/3. Since q is 7
modulo 9, take e=(q+2)/9, an integer with 3e=d+1.
If x is a nonzero cube then x^d=1, so (x^e)^3=x.
The same cube identity holds for x=0. The fixed root circuit
computes x^e with e=2 + 112*E_41(64). This exponent identity follows
from 64-1=63 and the remainder of m modulo 6. It starts with
three MAPs at tags 4, 5 and 6 on KEY, then two MULs to form
Y=x^112. It copies Y to Z and applies the geometric
steps for base beta=64 and target j=41, as specified
by the fixed macro sequence in construction.py. Multiply
the result by x^2 obtained by MAP with tag 1, leaving x^e in Z.
The decoder then emits the following validation and scoring.

    MAP(1,Z,T0)
    MUL(Z,T0,CHECK)
    Difference := 0
    for each literal word i in CHECK:
        xword := load(KEY+i)
        cword := load(CHECK+i)
        tword := xor(xword,cword)
        Difference := or(Difference,tword)
    IsBad := ltu(0,Difference)
    branch if IsBad to this tree's EXTEND
    SCORE(Z,KEY)
    MAP(omega,Z,T0)
    SCORE(T0,KEY)
    for each literal word i in Z:
        left := load(Z+i)
        right := load(T0+i)
        both := xor(left,right)
        store(T0+i,both)
    SCORE(T0,KEY)

SCORE is the same inline visit score action with the indicated
systematic and parity candidate arrays. The tested omega
satisfies omega^2=omega+1 and is neither zero nor one, so it is
a nontrivial cube root of unity. Upon passing validation with
nonzero x, the three roots are Z, omega*Z, and
(1+omega)*Z. They are distinct and exhaustive by the cubic
degree bound. For zero all three produce the same root,
which does not change decision or tie. If x is a noncube the
validation necessarily fails. The root work excluding scoring
includes the fixed ROOT_PROGRAM plus two MAPs, one MUL,
8k for comparison accumulation and the last XOR loop,
and two for the test and branch. There are at most three
score calls per visited parity candidate.

The declared verifier also follows the exact macro list on
formal exponents to check the cube identity, and checks
sample map-table expansions against actual field arithmetic.
This root circuit uses one COPY, 11 MAPs and 10 MULs
before the validation. Thus the two trees consider
exactly the union defined at the start, possibly with repetitions.
The smallest-distance and numerical-tie update ignores
repetitions consistently.

## Output and complete count

At the parity DONE label, for each k-word piece at BEST, load it
and pack it into the specified output interval for
the component. If its starting offset in its output
word is zero, store it directly there. Otherwise
shift it left by that offset, load the old output
word, OR, and store. If the live portion crosses a
boundary, shift the original piece right by 64
minus the offset, load the next output word, OR,
and store. This uses at most nine primitives per
piece. In the direct-store case, the word has no
earlier bits since pieces are processed in
increasing order. In every other case the live
positions are disjoint, so OR accumulates the output
without altering any earlier piece. The high bits of
the final word remain zero. Continue by fallthrough
to the next component's emitted initialization or
halt after the last. The code-generation loop over
components adds no runtime jump.

Let N_s=sum_(i<=Rs) binom(m,i) and N_p=sum_(i<=Rp) binom(m,i).
Both trees have one visit per increasing subset up to the cap.
For N visits, count N-1 successful extensions of 21 primitives,
N failed attempts of at most six, N-1 nonroot returns of
13 and one root return of two. These costs exclude the inserted
affine updates, root arithmetic and scoring. Their total is at
most 40N for each tree. The first tree's affine descent and
restoration are charged once per visit in the upper bound,
including the root. Write U for the proved affine update
cost and S=2*(2+3k) for the save and restore costs. Let
Dscore=18k+5. Write Mroot for the root work above, bounded
by 2k+11*Mmap+10*MUL_cost+2*Mmap+MUL_cost+8k+2.
The fixed initialization and packing cost at most 26k+2,
plus the first SQUARE and MUL. The count for all 4
components is bounded by

    W = 4*((U+S+Dscore+40)*N_s
           +(Mroot+3*Dscore+40)*N_p
           +26k+2+SQUARE_cost+MUL_cost)
      = 157186451944824355715068710708965957968284.

The exact comparison is W^250 <= 2^34213.
All field arrays and stacks lie in the same declared bank
and the MAP and arithmetic macros reuse at most 2k field
registers plus at most 24 scalar registers, within the
64-register budget. The primitive bound and deterministic
candidate rule hold for every possible received word.
